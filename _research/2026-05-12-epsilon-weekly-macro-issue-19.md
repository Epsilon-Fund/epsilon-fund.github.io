## Week In Review
US equities continued their strong performance, with the S&P 500 rising 2.4% and the Nasdaq 100 up 7.6% over the week, largely driven by AI-related sectors and robust corporate profits. Meanwhile, in Europe, German industry worsened due to the Middle East war, and Norges Bank delivered a hawkish rate hike, highlighting divergent regional economic and monetary policy trends.

## Geopolitics
Geopolitical concerns remained prominent, with German industry reportedly worsening due to the Middle East war, and Polish rates held steady amid rising regional risks. The uneasy US-Iran ceasefire, previously announced, saw oil prices initially fall but then creep up due to limited flow through the Strait of Hormuz and attacks on Saudi energy facilities.

Separately, the U.S.-China relationship continues to be characterized by 'competitive interdependence,' with deep economic and financial ties despite decoupling rhetoric. The U.S. remains reliant on China for strategic minerals, and China is a major export market and profit source for U.S. firms. Upcoming U.S.-China summits are expected to focus on tariffs, semiconductor restrictions, rare earth minerals, and Taiwan.

## Inflation
### United States
US headline CPI for April was reported at 3.3% YoY, with core inflation at 2.6% YoY. This follows March's 0.9% MoM increase in CPI, largely driven by a 10.9% rise in energy prices. Q1 2026 CPI inflation was 2.7% YoY, with projections for Q2 2026 indicating a rise to 4.2% YoY, suggesting persistent inflationary pressures.

### Europe
No major developments this week.
